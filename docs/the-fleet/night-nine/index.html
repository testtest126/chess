§ 1 — the rename

Named after the wrong Atari.

A working title is fine right up until it's the name on a public repository. This one had a trademark sitting inside it, and the fix took exactly the shape this fleet always reaches for: name it after what it actually is, the way the chess app already had.

The project started life as “Atari” — the Go term for a group down to its last liberty, not the company. On a public repo that distinction doesn't hold, so three days in it became GoMate, deliberately parallel to MateMate: the rules package, GoKit, kept its own name, since only the product needed to change; and atari itself got reserved in the project's own AGENTS.md as the in‑app term for exactly the situation it describes, where the Go word actually belongs.

The rename broke CI two minutes later. The SwiftPM cache key only hashed Package.swift/Package.resolved, so the runner restored a .build baked under the old checkout path, and swift test failed on a stale precompiled‑module mismatch — “missing required module 'SwiftShims'”. The fix added github.repository to the cache key, so the next rename, or the next fork, can't land in the same trap.

§ 2 — the build

A search engine that knows what it hasn't proven.

Four phases in a week: a tested rules core, a legal‑move bot, a first playable app, and finally a real search engine — each one landed with the same discipline this whole story keeps returning to.

01

GoKit, then a bot, then an app

Phase 1–3 · GoKit · GoProtocol · SwiftUI

GoKit shipped first and tested: board setup, liberties, capture, suicide rejection, ko via positional superko, area scoring. GoProtocol put a GoEngine seam behind it — a plain random mover, then a greedy one that takes captures, escapes atari, then plays for liberties, no search at all. Three days later, a SwiftUI iPhone app made all of it tappable: a warm‑wood goban, live capture counts, an end‑of‑game score with komi.

Ship the rules before the opponent, and the opponent before the interface. Each layer is easier to trust once the one under it already is.
02

MCTS arrives, and says exactly what it's for

Phase 4 · MCTS · UCT, robust‑child selection

The app's new default opponent runs standard UCT search — expand, playout, backpropagate, then play whichever move was visited most — 400 iterations, exploration constant √2, a fresh tree built from scratch every move. What ships with it is an honest strength read, not a marketing one: not benchmarked as a win‑rate. What's actually verified is a real tactical floor — at the shipping budget, it reliably finds an available capture a uniform‑random mover would land on well under half the time. It does not yet claim to outplay the simpler greedy bot head‑to‑head, because nobody ran that match.

A search engine is allowed to ship before it's the best player in the room. It is not allowed to say it is, until it's actually measured.

§ 3 — the sixth

The rules core outlived the app that made it.

GoMate is the sixth project this fleet has shipped under one hand, and the smallest sign of how settled that pattern has become is what happened next: its rules core didn't stay GoMate's alone. Palimpsest vendored GoKit in verbatim, months later, for a piece that has nothing to do with playing well and everything to do with a board that remembers. The same rules, doing two entirely different jobs, in two different repositories, because the rules were actually right the first time.

Renamed once, broken once, fixed within the hour, honest about what its own search engine hasn't proven yet. That's the whole shape of it.
